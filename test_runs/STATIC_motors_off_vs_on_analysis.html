Static IMU tests: motors off vs. motors on

Two runs with the IMU still: STATIC_0001 (37 min, wheels unpowered, 3 s gyro calibration) and STATIC_0005 (29 min, all four wheels held at 100 rpm, 60 s calibration). Every gyro sample was logged, with the raw magnetometer, accelerometer, wheel data and the onboard filter's output. The main question: can the magnetometer still serve as a heading reference with the motors running?

Magnetometer: conclusions

  • Running motors make the magnetometer much noisier, but almost all of that noise sits at a few specific frequencies. Field noise rose from 0.5/0.7/0.8 µT to 4.6/4.4/2.3 µT (x/y/z), and sample-to-sample heading scatter went from 0.7° to 5.5°. The spectrum shows sharp lines rather than broadband noise: one about 1000× above the noise floor near 4 Hz, and others at 10.3–10.4 Hz, which matches the motor-shaft rate at 100 rpm (100 × 6.25 / 60 = 10.4 Hz), plus 6.5, 8 and 14 Hz. The ~4 Hz line is most likely a faster motor-related ripple folded down by the magnetometer's ~100 Hz sampling.
  • Averaged over ~1 s or more, the heading stays steady with the motors at a constant speed. 30 s heading means vary by only 0.15° (std dev; 0.74° peak-to-peak) over 29 minutes, with a trend of -0.5 °/h. With the motors off it was 0.07°. The periodic noise filters out.
  • Starting the motors shifted the field and the heading. In the first 0.25 s, before the wheels turned, the field was (2.6, -43.9, -17.9) µT. Once all four wheels reached 100 rpm it was (-3.3, -48.4, -18.1) µT: a shift of (-5.9, -4.4, -0.2) µT, magnitude 47.5 → 52.1 µT, and a -7.6° heading jump. The horizontal component of Earth's field is only about 20 µT, so every µT of horizontal disturbance costs about 2–3° of heading. In closed-loop control the wheel speeds and currents change constantly, so this offset would keep changing too.
  • The raw magnetometer is dominated by the table's own magnetic fields. The field dips only 27–23° below horizontal, where Earth's field here dips about 65°. Magnitude is 45–53 µT. Large fixed offsets, probably from the motors' permanent magnets and steel parts, are mixed in. For heading on a rotating table these must be calibrated out by turning the table through a full circle about z. A DEMO SPIN does exactly that.
  • Verdict on the planned magnetometer yaw aid: feasible, with three conditions. (1) a hard-iron calibration from a full turn; (2) low-pass or notch filtering of the 4–14 Hz motor lines; (3) either a model of the offset versus each wheel's speed and current, or only trusting the magnetometer when the wheels are near the state it was calibrated in. Without (3), heading errors of several degrees appear whenever the wheels change speed, so it can't meet ±1° alone. It could still cap long-term drift to a few degrees.

Other conclusions

  • Yaw drift stayed high (-24 °/h) mainly because the gyro was calibrated while still warming up. Both runs began about 2 minutes after power-on, and the filter started from a z-bias near −0.0125 °/s, the value from a calibration at power-on. The true z-gyro average then moved to about −0.019 °/s within ~5 minutes as the chip warmed (a shift of ~0.007 °/s ≈ 25 °/h), and sat there with some minute-to-minute wander. Replay check: both logs were re-run through a copy of the firmware's filter. With the old settings from the logged start state it reproduces the logged drift (−27.4 vs −27.1 °/h). Calibrating after 10 minutes of warm-up instead gives about −3 °/h (motors-off run). My earlier reading that the filter had dragged the bias off a good calibration was mostly wrong. From a good start, the old settings only moved it slightly (−0.0180 → −0.0169 °/s over 36 min). The bias settings have still been tightened and the z-bias locked to the calibration, which holds it exactly.
  • The gyro is unaffected by the motors. Allan deviation is identical: about 50 °/h at 1 s, with a floor of 1.9 °/h on (vs 2.4 °/h off).
  • Vibration is visible but harmless. Accelerometer x/y noise rose about 4–5× (0.017/0.016 → 0.070/0.084 m/s², around 11–13 Hz). No accelerometer update was rejected, and roll/pitch std dev only went from 0.02/0.02° to 0.05/0.04°.
  • All four wheels held 100.0 rpm (±3) at nearly identical duty (0.096, 0.095, 0.096, 0.097 for A–D). At this speed wheels A and C show none of their earlier extra load or oscillation. Earlier runs were at about 480 rpm actual (300 in the old scaling), so those problems may be speed-dependent.
  • Sample loss is steady at about 26%, and the long freezes persist. The BNO085's sequence numbers show 45,813 of 173,448 gyro reports never reached the Teensy (26.4%), the same rate as with SD logging off after boot. That points to the missing INT-pin handling. There were 43 freezes over 100 ms (max 321 ms), as in the motors-off run. Most showed no sequence jump, which fits the BNO085 holding and retrying a report while the Teensy is blocked, for example by an SD write. A longer SD-off idle check is still needed to confirm the SD card is the cause.

Recommendations

  1. Warm up before calibrating (procedure, no code): power on, wait ~10 min, then CALIBRATE 180 (now takes a duration). Replay predicts about 3 °/h. The filter's bias settings are also tightened, and the z-bias is locked to the calibration (done).
  2. Map the motor magnetic offset (test): step all wheels, then each wheel alone, through 0, ±50, ±100, ±200 rpm, holding each about 20 s. Log the averaged field per state, to see whether the offset can be modelled from wheel speed and current or only gated.
  3. Hard-iron calibration from a full turn: fit the horizontal field circle during a DEMO SPIN (wheels at constant speed while spinning) and store the offset.
  4. Then decide on the magnetometer aid: low-pass at ~1 Hz (or notch 4 / 10.4 Hz), use it only when the wheels are steady, and with a large measurement noise so it trims drift rather than steering heading.
  5. Unchanged priorities from TODO.md: wire BNO085 INT (pin 20) and RST (pin 21) for the steady 26% loss and the post-upload hang; non-blocking SD writes for the >100 ms freezes. Both matter before closed-loop DEMO SPIN runs, because a freeze over 100 ms disarms the wheels.
