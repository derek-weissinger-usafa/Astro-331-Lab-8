Setup

  • Table fixed (clamped), moved away from a steel tool cabinet after a first run (MAGMAP_0002) next to it; that run is not used.
  • Schedule (MAG MAP, started over the XBee, logged to SD at 100 Hz): all four wheels 0, ±50, ±100, ±200 rpm, then each wheel alone +100, +200, 0, −100, −200, 0 rpm; 20 s per step, 33 steps, 11.6 min, 69,512 rows.
  • Averaging: each step's mean field uses its last ~14 s (the first 6 s cover the 20 rad/s² ramp and settling).
  • Data quality: no SD rows dropped; IMU lost 68 gyro samples (normal rate), no chip resets, longest gap 70 ms. All four wheels stayed in service; wheel D's intermittent encoder lead held for the whole run.
  • Heading conversion: degrees are quoted as the aid sees them. It works on the raw field, whose horizontal part reads 37.7 µT here, so 1 µT across it ≈ 1.5°. (Owen's notes use 2.7°/µT from a 21.5 µT horizontal field; see §4 for why the raw field is larger now.)

1 Wheel model: direction step and speed slope

The aid's model per wheel and body axis (mag_aid.cpp): field = constant + direction step × sign(speed) + speed slope × (|speed| − 10.47 rad/s). Fitted to each wheel's four spinning steps (±100, ±200 rpm) with the other three wheels stopped and unmoved, so their fields cancel out of the fit.

2 Stopped wheels: the field depends on where each shaft stops

This confirms Owen's finding and the aid's design: a stopped reference is only valid for the exact shaft positions it was learned at (encoder counts within 1), and is re-learned at every new stop. The magnet field of a stopped motor is several times larger than the direction step of a spinning one.

Earth's field here is about 52 µT. The magnetometer now reads 64 µT, so at least ~12 µT is an offset from magnetic material near the IMU, and it has changed with the hardware or the location (new Teensy, rewiring, table position). With the table fixed, an offset that moves with the table can't be separated from the room's own field.

This doesn't matter for holding a heading: references are learned in place. It does matter as soon as the table yaws (DEMO SPIN, slews, or drifting away from the learned heading): a table-fixed offset turns with the table while the Earth field doesn't, so the heading the aid computes is distorted, roughly by (offset ÷ field) × the yaw change — potentially several degrees for a 10° turn. The hard-iron calibration (Owen's item 9: a full yaw turn on the air bearing, least-squares fit) should come before any air-bearing test with yaw motion.

Also seen: the yaw estimate drifted -3.9° over the 11.6 min (~20 °/h, aid mostly gated in this schedule). The gyro was calibrated straight after power-on, before the ~10 min warm-up — the known cause. Not part of the wheel calibration.

Practical effect is small: each direction pattern has its own learned reference, so the direction step is constant within a pattern and absorbed. Only the speed slopes act during a run (≤ 0.4° over 50–200 rpm). The update mainly puts wheel D in the model and records the current hardware.
