Magnetometer disturbance map (MAG MAP)

MAGMAP_0001.CSV: table fixed, started 15 min after power-on (after warm-up and calibration). 33 steps × 20 s: all wheels at 0, ±50, ±100, ±200 rpm, then each wheel alone at ±100, ±200 rpm, with all wheels stopped between groups, then 3 min stopped. Each step is averaged over its settled part (6–19.5 s). Offsets are measured against the neighbouring all-stopped steps. Heading here is the raw uncalibrated magnetometer heading, so only its changes are meaningful.

What the data shows

  • The biggest disturbance is the motors' permanent magnets, and it depends on where each shaft stops. With every wheel stopped, the field is steady to 0.17 µT. After a wheel spins and stops again, the stopped field jumps by 0.9–5.1 µT for one wheel (wheel C moved it 5.1 µT once), and 7.2–9.5 µT when all four stop. Each motor's rotor magnets, and especially the encoder's magnet ring on the shaft, add a field that depends on shaft angle. Over the 11-minute test the 'wheels stopped' field moved 9 µT in x and 11 µT in y, and its heading moved 9°. So there is no single 'motors off' calibration: it changes every time a wheel stops.
  • While spinning, the magnets add fast noise rather than an offset. Heading noise is 4.6–7.5° sample to sample (0.6° stopped), at shaft-related frequencies: at −50 rpm the 5.2 Hz shaft line is visible directly, at ±100 rpm the 10.4 Hz line. Most of this averages away within a second. The danger is low wheel speed: near zero rpm the same magnets swing the field slowly, and slow changes can't be filtered out.
  • The average field while spinning differs from the stopped field by up to 4.0° of heading (rms 1.6°), but not in a clean pattern. There's no consistent scaling with speed or direction, and the single-wheel effects don't add up to the all-wheel effect (mismatch up to 8 µT). That's expected, given that every stopped reference it's compared against was itself random (finding 1). A per-wheel model of speed and |speed| leaves 1.7 µT rms (≈ 1.8°) unexplained, so a correction model from this test isn't reliable.
  • Vibration peaks at 50 rpm. Accelerometer noise is about 3× higher at ±50 rpm (≈ 0.10 m/s²) than at 100–200 rpm (≈ 0.03 m/s²). A structural resonance is likely near there, so avoid holding wheels around 50 rpm.
  • Yaw drifted -14 °/h even after warm-up calibration. The z-bias was locked at -0.0216 °/s, as intended, but the gyro averaged -0.0257 °/s during the test, and still -0.0256 °/s with the wheels stopped afterwards. So the bias moved by about 0.004 °/s after calibration, independent of wheel speed. Likely thermal: the motors and packs running for ~15 minutes near the board, or warm-up continuing. Recalibrating just before each run, with the motor packs on, should help.
  • Sample loss continues: 22,276 gyro reports never reached the Teensy during the test, the same ~26% as before. The INT-pin fix is still pending.

Recommendations

  1. Keep the wheels spinning whenever the magnetometer matters. Use the 4-wheel null mode (kNullBiasRadS, currently 0) to hold all four wheels at about 100 rpm, which avoids the 50 rpm resonance. Then no shaft sits at a random stopped angle and nobody crosses zero speed slowly. The magnet fields become fast noise that a ~1 s low-pass filter removes, and the remaining offset is a single repeatable state.
  2. Calibrate the magnetometer in that same state: do the full-turn hard-iron calibration during a DEMO SPIN with the wheels at the bias speed, not stopped.
  3. To build a correction model, re-map with every wheel kept spinning: start all at +100 rpm and step one wheel at a time through 50–200 rpm, never stopping. A MAG MAP v2 schedule is a small change if you want it.
  4. Realistic expectation: even with these steps, a few µT (a couple of degrees) of uncertainty will likely remain, so a magnetometer aid can cap long-term yaw drift rather than give ±1°. A magnet's field falls off as 1/distance³, so moving the IMU twice as far from the motors cuts the disturbance about 8×. That's the only fix that attacks the source.
  5. Gyro: recalibrate (CALIBRATE 180) just before each yaw-critical run with the motor packs already on, and keep runs short. Drift was −14 °/h here (≈ 1° per 4 min).
