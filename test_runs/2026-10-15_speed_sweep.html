Wheel speed sweep: commanded vs measured

2026-10-15. All four wheels were sent SPEED ALL every 0.1 s over USB: hold 0 for 2 s, a cosine ramp from 0 to 300 rpm over 10 s, hold 3 s, ramp back to 0 over 10 s, then KILL. Measured speed is the firmware's USB telemetry at 20 Hz. In Manual mode this is the low-passed encoder speed (τ = 0.1 s) that the speed loop itself uses.

What the data shows

  • Every wheel reaches and holds 300 rpm on average (hold means 299.7–300.5 rpm), and the measured speed follows the commanded ramps with no visible lag at 20 Hz.
  • Delayed start, all four wheels together: no wheel moves until the command reaches 29 rpm (t = 4.0 s). They all break away in the same sample and then jump to catch up, which is the largest error of the run (≈ 25 rpm). All four get the same command and gains, so they get the same duty, and that duty stays below the motors' breakaway duty for about 2 s. At low speed the feedforward (command ÷ 88 rad/s) and the PI gains (Kp 0.009, Ki 0.045) produce only a few percent duty. Static-friction compensation (a minimum duty when the command is non-zero) or a larger Ki would shorten this. On the way down the wheels track all the way to 0.
  • Wheels A and C oscillate while holding (SD ≈ 5–6 rpm, swings up to ±16 rpm). B and D stay within ±1 rpm. The four share gains and firmware, so the difference is in the hardware (wheel balance, encoder signal, motor, or pack). Swapping a pack or encoder lead between A and B would separate the causes.
